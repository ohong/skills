## Choosing a composition

Compare alternatives using the subject's actual meaning, not its industry label.
A software project can become a garden when cultivation explains its behavior; technology alone does not justify garden imagery.

- **Interlocking field:** Distinct patterned objects form one larger whole. Give each object a role within the project's metaphor.
- **Close-up world:** A small environment reveals many nested relationships. Preserve a few large shapes so fine detail remains legible.
- **Journey:** An avenue, path, or repeated procession gives the eye a destination. Let activity show what progress means.
- **Working landscape:** Different actions connect through paths and shared space. Show relationships without diagram labels.
- **Repeated motif:** One family of shapes creates rhythm, with individual differences and a clear change in density.
- **Connected vignettes:** Several small scenes express a cycle or shared theme. Use a continuous visual element to unify them.

For example, a reading project could use a patterned field of books, or a landscape where people exchange stories.
The field emphasizes variety; the landscape emphasizes relationships. Choose based on the project's purpose.

Borrow structure and visual craft, then invent the scene and details.
The historical mastheads, printed prices, dates, signatures, and scan wear are not default output elements.
