# Sources and editorial choices

Steven Pinker’s *The Sense of Style* (2014) supplies the editorial principles. The six chapters move from close reading and classic style through the curse of knowledge, syntax, coherence, and usage. The following sources give a maintainer places to check particular decisions.

- **Voice and pleasure.** Pinker studies passages that work, down to the detail or rhythm that makes them memorable. This informs the instruction to identify a draft’s strengths before editing it. [Publisher’s excerpt](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.ca/books/310859/the-sense-of-style-by-steven-pinker/9780143127796/excerpt)
- **Classic style and the curse of knowledge.** Direct the reader’s attention to the subject. An expert can forget which steps a newcomer needs to see. [Why Academics Stink at Writing](https://stevenpinker.com/files/pinker/files/pinker_2014_why_academics_writing_stinks.pdf)
- **Syntax.** Peter DeScioli and Steven Pinker examine the cost of packing modifiers before a noun. Prepositions can make those relationships explicit. Pinker’s discussion of the passive explains how the choice of subject directs attention. [Piled modifiers](https://stevenpinker.com/files/pinker/files/descioli_pinker_2021_piled_modifiers.pdf) · [Passive Resistance](https://stevenpinker.com/files/pinker/files/documents/passive-resistance.pdf)
- **Coherence.** Readers track a topic across sentences and infer relationships between ideas. Ordering, repetition, and an occasional explicit connective help them do so. [Arcs of Coherence, chapter excerpt](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/teaching/1617/R216/arcs_of_coherence.pdf)
- **Usage.** Pinker tests familiar prescriptions against their purpose, the grammar of English, and actual usage. [Pinker on ten disputed grammar rules](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2014/aug/15/steven-pinker-10-grammar-rules-break)

The five editing steps, delivery instructions, examples, and evaluation cases are adaptations for agents. The examples are original.
